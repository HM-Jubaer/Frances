The Operating
Report.

A new visual identity for Frances, separate from her current site. It is modelled on the documents she spent 30 years reading inside Bosch, Cummins and Eaton: annual reports, engineering drawings and operating reviews. It is precise and printed, and it does not try to charm.

01Concept

Diagnosis looks like a document.

Her work is reading systems. So the site reads like one: a strict grid, hairline rules, tables instead of cards, and one signal colour that means one thing. Profit Pulse™ is that signal.

  • Ruled, not boxedContent sits on hairlines, the way a report does.
  • Left-aligned and asymmetricA 3/9 column split, with the section number in the margin.
  • Signal colour is rationedVermilion only appears on Profit Pulse™.
  • Real information as the visualTables, scales and checklists rather than decoration.
  • Monochrome photographyIt flatters the low-res portrait and suits the print feel.
  • Square corners everywhereZero border radius.
02Colour

Paper, ink and one signal.

This deliberately drops the navy and peach. Use roughly 80% paper, 18% ink and 2% signal. If vermilion appears on something that is not Profit Pulse™, it is wrong.

03Typography

Archivo, with Newsreader for voices.

Archivo is an industrial grotesque with a width axis. It runs condensed and heavy for headlines and at normal width for reading. Newsreader is used only when a person is speaking: testimonials and pull quotes. These replace Source Serif and PT Sans completely.

04Grid & rules

Twelve columns. Margin notes.

The container is 1240px with a 12-column grid and a 32px gutter. Every section uses a 3/9 split, with the section number and label in the 3-column margin. Sections are separated by a 1px rule line, and the most important ones by a 2px ink line.

Vertical rhythm: 8, 16, 24, 32, 56, 96, 144. Section padding is 96px on desktop and 64px on mobile.

05Actions

Rectangles, plainly labelled.

Buttons have no pills, no arrow circles and no lift on hover. The label says what happens.

08Photography

Monochrome, captioned.

Every image is grayscale with a figure caption. It hides the low resolution of the current portrait and stays consistent once the new shoot arrives. Brief for the shoot: working environments, hands on documents, whiteboards and plant floors, with no posed handshakes and no smiling at laptops.

09What we avoid

The template tells.

These patterns make a site look generated, or look like the template it came from. None of them appear in Design B.

  • Tiny uppercase eyebrow labels with sparkles over every heading
  • Centred heading-and-subtitle stacks for every section
  • Pill buttons with an arrow in a circle
  • Floating stat "chips" around a portrait
  • Gradient glows, glass panels, grid-paper backgrounds
  • A 3×2 grid of icon-in-rounded-square cards
  • Cards that lift on hover
  • Coloured italic words inside headlines
  • Pricing tiers with a "Most popular" ribbon
  • Logo marquees and testimonial carousels with stars
  • Decorative dashboards and gauges with fake data
  • Blob shapes, hatched circles, morphing masks
